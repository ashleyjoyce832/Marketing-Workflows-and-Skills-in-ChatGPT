# Five-minute speaker script

Slides 1–8 form the main talk. Slides 9–18 are optional appendix material. Timings are rehearsal targets, not measured speaking times. Source citations appear in the slide notes and sources.md.

## Slide 1: Marketing workflows with ChatGPT (0:00–0:25)

I wanted to explore how ChatGPT could help marketers work more efficiently while keeping human creativity central. I used a hypothetical Werner Bronkhorst wallpaper launch as my case study. What I learned came from the back-and-forth: defining the idea, seeing what the model misunderstood, and turning those corrections into reusable workflow instructions.

## Slide 2: My ideas came first (0:25–1:05)

I began by reading about marketing workflows. Claude helped organize the project, and I developed the original ideas on paper, using questions about my goals and vision. The creativity reading recommends keeping humans responsible for early ideation because AI assistance can make different people’s outputs more similar. My rule for ChatGPT was specific: ask questions and pressure-test my thinking before generating directions. That was my chosen working method. It helped preserve the purpose behind the idea: amplifying artists and human talent.

## Slide 3: Pressure-testing narrowed the brief (1:05–1:45)

My first idea grew into a much bigger exhibition and creative event. Through questioning, I narrowed it to one proposed paid hotel collaboration, with consumer wallpaper afterward. The useful challenge was: why would the hotel pay? Social attention alone would not answer that. I had to separate the hotel buyer, the event visitor, and the wallpaper customer. We chose The Fifth Avenue Hotel as a hypothetical prospect. Its interest, the product details, and the economics still need real validation. Times Square belongs to the later wallpaper launch.

## Slide 4: Specific feedback improved the work (1:45–2:25)

The concept then became New York Minute: Broadway Backstage, Stories on the Streets, and Five Minutes on Fifth Ave. Visual generation made the idea tangible, but my feedback made it more specific. I pushed for tiny figures, finer paint lines, a green-and-cream Fifth Avenue palette, and settings beyond a bedroom. We tried rooms, ceilings, rolls, and billboard concepts. I could see the direction improving, but we did not measure time saved or commercial performance. A polished image was evidence of a creative iteration, not evidence that customers would buy.

## Slide 5: The feed needed more of the artist (2:25–3:05)

Today’s biggest correction was about native content. The first ads looked polished, but I wanted more of the rhythm in Werner’s actual feed: standalone artwork, close-up texture, and the making process, with a campaign reveal occasionally. I supplied screenshots and asked for that feeling. The revised sequence follows one small painting through a process shot and a Times Square reveal. These are AI-generated proposals. If this campaign went live, the process content would need real footage of the artist making the work, and the partnerships would need agreement.

## Slide 6: Corrections became skill instructions (3:05–3:50)

A useful skill saves how we want the work done. For example, the ad workflow should inspect my references before writing variations. The concept workflow should preserve my idea, and it should flag generic elements only when they are actually present. My practical tip is to complete a task in chat, correct the output, and then ask ChatGPT to turn the successful process into a SKILL.md. Include the inputs, steps, output format, and quality checks. Then try it on a different brief. Saving a file does not prove the skill works reliably.

## Slide 7: Once live, the workflow changes (3:50–4:35)

After launch, I would use different skills. Channel tracking would separate each source and creative version. An experiment plan would define one change, a primary outcome, and a decision rule before the test. Channel management would check links, availability, approvals, and customer questions. A performance digest would distinguish hotel inquiries from wallpaper orders and social engagement. It would also flag missing data and uncertain results. Synthetic reactions can surface questions, but they cannot validate demand. And a QR scan can show response to a billboard without proving that the billboard caused extra sales.

## Slide 8: What I would repeat (4:35–5:00)

My method is originate, run, and steer. Bring a human idea and real references, use ChatGPT for a specific task, then make the judgment calls. The repository separates workflows we exercised from new skills drafted for launch. A small business could start with one recurring task and a simple review log. The next proof would be better work with less rework, measured over repeated use.

## Optional appendix talking points

### Slide 9: Appendix: the tools serve different roles

Use a skill for a repeatable task. Add automation or an agent only when the trigger, data access, permitted actions, and failure handling are clear. A scheduled workflow can follow fixed steps. Agent autonomy is a separate design choice.

### Slide 10: Building a skill in chat

Give the creator a successful example and the corrections that mattered. Ask it to preserve current authorization, identify missing inputs, and describe the deliverable. Keep campaign-specific facts in a brief, rather than embedding them as universal instructions. Use the build-skills guide for the installation route available in your product.

### Slide 11: What we exercised in this project

The conversations demonstrate these workflow behaviors. They do not establish that every output came from the final saved skill version. Handmade process planning supports the creative work, but the supplied process pictures are generated concepts. Synthetic testing, tracking, and performance reporting remain proposed live-stage workflows.

### Slide 12: New skills for the live phase

These five additions turn the library into a full preparation and learning workflow. They produce reviewable documents, not automatic permission to publish, contact customers, or change spend. The repository includes inputs, output contracts, boundaries, and a small evaluation plan for each.

### Slide 13: Testing the campaign

For a wallpaper launch, first validate whether samples and fulfillment actually exist. Then compare two approved copy angles with the same image, audience eligibility, placement, offer, and time period. Prespecify sample needs, baseline, minimum meaningful effect, budget, and stopping rules. Avoid choosing a winner after a few favorable clicks. For qualitative research, ask what people understand and what prevents purchase.

### Slide 14: Measurement follows the business question

Keep the denominators and time windows consistent. Reconcile purchases and refunds with the commerce record, not just ad-platform totals. Distinguish sample orders from full wallpaper purchases. For a billboard, use a unique landing page or QR code as a response signal. A credible holdout or other suitable causal design would be needed to estimate incrementality. One location with no control cannot support that claim.

### Slide 15: A weekly operating rhythm

Channel operations should maintain one source of truth for asset versions and approvals. Log what actually ran, its URL, time zone, owner, and tracking ID. Review cross-channel repetition and creative fatigue without assuming a performance dip proves fatigue. Route recurring installation questions into truthful FAQs or lifecycle content. Escalate product or fulfillment issues to the owner.
