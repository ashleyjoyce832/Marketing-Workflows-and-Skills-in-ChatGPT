# New York Minute: decision trail

## What this case can establish

It records a creative workflow, selected artifacts, and human corrections. It does not demonstrate incremental sales, measured productivity gains, or validated consumer demand. This is a selective synthesis of accessible project chats and saved records, not a complete export of every ChatGPT conversation.

## Sequence

1. **Read and plan.** Ashley read workflow material, used Claude to organize the project, and developed her goals and vision on paper. The purpose was to amplify artists and human talent while using AI for supporting work.
2. **Originate.** The initial wallpaper/exhibition idea included a Times Square painting reveal. A larger Met/Flowstate creative event also appeared in early brainstorming. Ashley later removed that initiative from the current deliverables.
3. **Pressure-test.** ChatGPT asked about the primary buyer, the difference between event attention and purchase intent, and the hotel's reason to pay. An expensive hotel stay did not establish wallpaper demand. The teaching goal also limited how far production logistics should take over the work.
4. **Narrow the business sequence.** Ashley selected one hypothetical paid hotel collaboration first. Additional hotel collaborations would depend on evidence. Consumer wallpaper would come afterward. The later billboard would amplify that wallpaper phase. Partner willingness, reproduction rights, costs, product facts, and economics remain unresolved.
5. **Expand the creative world.** The artwork became a three-design New York Minute collection. The saved decision trail records changes from bedrooms toward lobby/ballroom settings, large motifs toward tiny figures, and general city imagery toward Broadway, street life, and Fifth Avenue details.
6. **Make alternatives visible.** Image-generation iterations explored paint detail, product rolls, room and ceiling installations, a hotel event, and Times Square scenes. Naming helped connect the collection. These are concept studies, not manufactured wallpaper or event photographs.
7. **Correct channel fit.** Ashley supplied screenshots of the artist's feed and asked for native art and process content. The later sequence showed a small painting, the making detail, and a city-scale reveal. The profile grid mixed isolated art, detail, process, and occasional campaign content. No performance claim follows from that visual preference.
8. **Distill the workflow.** The repository preserves these lessons as inputs, boundaries, checks, and outputs. It also adds five launch-stage skills from the readings. The final files have structural checks, not a live validation history.

## Human decisions versus AI contribution

Ashley supplied the originating purpose, artist reference, initial campaign idea, strategic choices, taste judgments, and revision requests. AI organized inputs, asked questions, supported research, expanded approved directions, produced mockups, and drafted reusable instructions. Avoid attributing the entire collection or every detail exclusively to either party.
